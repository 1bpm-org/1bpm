How to sit

There is no correct religion for this, and no incorrect one. The guidelines are about attention and the body. Use them until you do not need them.

  1. Pick a duration before you start.

    Ten minutes is the session. Twenty is the longer one. Do not renegotiate in the middle. When the time ends, stop.

  2. Let the body be sustainable.

    Sit, stand, or walk slowly. Feet on the floor, or folded only if the knees will last the full ten minutes. Hands rest. Jaw unclenched. Eyes open with a soft gaze, or closed if that does not cause sleep. Lying down is allowed if sitting hurts; sleep is rest, not a failed sit.

    Do not chase a photogenic posture. Sharp pain, numbness, or joint strain means move. Ordinary stillness-ache can be watched. Scratch what itches. Shift, then return.

  3. One anchor, not a blank mind.

    Breath at the nostrils or the belly, or the feet on the floor, or the next step if walking. When attention leaves, notice and come back. That return is the practice. Thoughts may stay in the room. You do not have to follow them or replace them.

  4. Do not manage the breath unless it is the anchor.

    Feel it; do not perform it. A few slower cycles at a very agitated start are allowed, then stop controlling. A full-sit breathing technique is a different exercise: allowed, not required, not more advanced.

  5. Kindness is practical.

    When you notice you left, do not add a punishment. The sentence is only: Back.

  6. You do not need an experience.

    No warmth, vision, insight, tears, or calm is required. If the sit was boring or scattered, it was real. Do not hunt calm.

  7. Ending.

    Do not leap. Feel the room for a few breaths. In a group, let silence stay a moment. Do not ask what people saw.

Same time tomorrow is enough. The other bell exists if this one is in the middle of your night.
