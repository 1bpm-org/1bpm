When it is hard

  • I can’t stop thinking.

    You are not supposed to. Come back. Counting the breath from 1 to 10, then restarting, is a legal aid. Losing count means start at 1. Do not keep score.

  • I got sleepy.

    Open the eyes. Sit up. Feel the feet. If the body needs sleep, nap, and use the other bell. Do not win against exhaustion.

  • I felt worse.

    Shorten to five minutes. Open the eyes. Feel the chair and name three things you can see.

    If practice regularly leaves you worse, stop and speak to a clinician. Do not push through. This is not treatment.

    If you are in danger now, call your local emergency number. In many countries, findahelpline.com lists a free, confidential line near you.

  • I need a special morning.

    You do not. A parked car or a break room is enough.

  • I missed the bell.

    Sit later, or wait for the other one. There is no streak.

  • I want it deeper.

    Longer sits are yours to try later. Depth is not a sensation. Hunting it usually means leaving the anchor.

  • I am in pain.

    Move. Sharp pain, chest pain, or trouble breathing: stop. That is medical.

  • I don’t know if I’m doing it right.

    A chosen duration, one anchor, and a return when you notice you left. That is the whole test.

The best instruction is the shortest one you will still remember at minute eight: Back.
